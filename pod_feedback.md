# Pod Feedback — Week 5 Skeptical Director Role-Play

**Analyst:** Kelvin Moruri
**Pod:** Oil & Gas
**Format:** Peer role-play — each member presented the Nairobi Depot findings for 5 minutes; pod members alternated playing a "Skeptical Director."

## Questions I Received

1. **"Our operations have normal ups and downs every month — how do you know this isn't just routine fluctuation?"**
   Pushed me to lead with the 7-day rolling average chart instead of raw daily numbers, since the raw data alone made it easy to dismiss as noise.

2. **"Couldn't this just be the weather? February can be unpredictable."**
   Made me realize I needed to state the correlation coefficient (r ≈ 0.01) out loud, not just show the heatmap, since a director skimming a chart won't necessarily register "near zero" on their own.

3. **"Why Nairobi specifically — could this be a data collection error at that depot?"**
   Good catch. I hadn't explicitly addressed data quality in my presentation. I added a line noting that missing-value checks came back clean and that the anomaly is a sustained shift, not a data gap.

4. **"What's the cost of sending an emergency crew if you're wrong?"**
   I didn't have a strong answer for this in the first run-through. On the second pass I reframed the recommendation around cost of inaction (accumulating throughput loss) versus cost of a short inspection visit, rather than just asserting the team should go.

## How I Improved My Argument

- **Led with the timeline chart, not the summary stats** — visual evidence of the break landed better than a table of numbers.
- **Said the correlation numbers out loud** instead of assuming the heatmap colors would speak for themselves.
- **Added an explicit data-quality check** ("no missing values, all four depots equally sampled") before presenting the root cause, to pre-empt the "is this even real data" objection.
- **Reframed the ask around cost of inaction vs. cost of action**, which made the recommendation feel proportionate rather than alarmist.

## Feedback I Gave Others

For a teammate presenting a Retail stock-out scenario, I pushed on whether they'd ruled out a confounding variable (a regional promotion driving demand) before blaming the supplier — a nudge to explicitly test alternative explanations, not just the first hypothesis that fits.

## Key Takeaway

The strongest defense against pushback wasn't having more charts — it was anticipating *which* objection was coming and having the specific number ready before the director asked for it.
